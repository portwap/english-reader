"Is this seat free?" asked the old man. "Yes, please sit down," said Tom.

The train was full. It was raining outside, and the windows were grey.

"Where are you going?" the old man asked. "To the coast," Tom said. "My sister lives there."

"I used to live by the sea," said the old man. "Many years ago. I was a fisherman."

Tom looked at his hands. They were big and brown. "Was it hard work?"

"Very hard. We started at four in the morning. Sometimes the sea was angry."

"Were you ever afraid?" "Every day," the old man laughed. "Only fools are never afraid."

The train stopped at a small station. Nobody got on. Nobody got off.

"Why did you stop?" Tom asked. "My boat was old, and so was I," said the man.

"Do you miss it?" "I miss the mornings. The water is silver before the sun comes up."

A woman with a baby walked past them. The baby was asleep. Its mouth was open.

"Do you have children?" Tom asked. "Two sons. They live in the city now."

"Do they visit you?" "At Christmas. They are busy people."

The old man took a photo from his pocket. It showed a small blue boat on a beach.

"That's her," he said. "Her name was Molly." "After your wife?" "After my mother."

Tom smiled. "It's a beautiful boat." "She was slow, but she always came home."

The rain stopped. Suddenly the sun was shining on the wet fields.

"Look," said the old man. "You can see the sea from here." Tom turned to the window.

There it was: a thin line of blue between the hills. "I see it," he said.

"Every time I see it, I feel young again," said the old man. "Just for a minute."

"Are you going to the coast too?" Tom asked. "Yes. I'm going to see Molly."

"Your boat? Is she still there?" "Yes. A young man bought her. He lets me visit."

"Does he still fish with her?" "Every morning. At four o'clock, like me."

The train began to slow down. People stood up and took their bags.

"This is my stop," said Tom. "It was nice to meet you." "And you, young man."

"Say hello to Molly," Tom said. The old man laughed. "I will. She remembers everyone."
